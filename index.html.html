A history in seven levels

From oscilloscope screens to open worlds, here are the big milestones.

  1. 1958

    Tennis for Two

    Physicist William Higinbotham builds a tennis game shown on an oscilloscope to entertain visitors to an American laboratory.

  2. 1972

    Pong

    Atari releases Pong. Two paddles, one ball: arcade games move into bars and pizza parlors.

  3. 1985

    Super Mario Bros.

    On the NES, Nintendo revives the market after the 1983 crash and makes the platformer a household genre.

  4. 1994

    PlayStation

    Sony joins the race with 3D games on CD, reaching an older audience.

  5. 2007

    The smartphone

    With the iPhone, games fit in your pocket. Millions of people start playing without ever thinking of themselves as “gamers.”

  6. 2011

    Minecraft

    Mojang’s building game becomes one of the best-selling games of all time.

  7. 2017

    Switch

    Nintendo offers a console you can play on the couch or on the go. The same year, Fortnite popularizes the free-to-play game with seasons.

And in the USA?

The United States is home to major publishers and many influential studios.

1993 · id Software

Doom

Made in Texas, it helped make the first-person shooter a mainstream genre and spread online multiplayer.

2004 · Blizzard Entertainment

World of Warcraft

A California studio’s online role-playing world, where millions of players adventure together.

2013 · Naughty Dog

The Last of Us

A story-driven action game from a California studio, praised for its characters and emotional writing.
